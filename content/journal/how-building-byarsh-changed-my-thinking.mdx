For a long time, I believed a website had to do everything.

It had to be fast. Beautiful. Full of animations. Packed with features. Optimised for every trend. Every time I opened another portfolio or personal website, I felt the pressure to add one more section, one more effect, one more reason for someone to stay.

But somewhere during the process of building ByArsh, my thinking changed.

I realised that the internet doesn't always need another loud website.

Sometimes it needs a quiet one.

> The internet doesn't always need another loud website. Sometimes it needs a quiet one.

That single thought slowly became the foundation of everything I was building.

Instead of asking, "What else can I add?", I started asking, "What can I remove?"

The answer was surprising.

I removed unnecessary animations. I simplified layouts. I stopped designing pages to impress people and started designing them to make someone feel comfortable.

ByArsh slowly transformed from a developer portfolio into a digital journal.

A place where software engineering could exist alongside books, travel stories, quiet reflections, and everyday learning.

That balance felt more authentic than separating my professional life from my personal interests.

As I continued building, I realised another important lesson.

The internet remembers everything we publish.

If that is true, I wanted my small corner of it to represent the person I genuinely hope to become—not just the developer I am today.

That meant choosing thoughtful writing over constant posting.

Quality over quantity.

Consistency over perfection.

And honesty over trends.

Building ByArsh also reminded me that growth isn't always visible.

Many evenings ended with nothing more than adjusting typography, rewriting a paragraph, or moving a single section a few pixels higher.

None of those changes were dramatic.

But together they shaped the experience I wanted someone to have when visiting this website.

Perhaps the biggest lesson wasn't about design or development at all.

It was about creating something with patience.

The internet moves incredibly fast.

New technologies appear every month.

Design trends change almost every season.

Algorithms reward speed.

But meaningful work rarely happens quickly.

ByArsh became my reminder that it's perfectly acceptable to build slowly, as long as you're building intentionally.

Today, I no longer think of this website as a portfolio.

I think of it as a collection of everything I'm learning.

The projects I build.

The books that challenge my thinking.

The places I travel.

The mistakes I make.

The ideas that stay with me.

I hope that years from now, when I look back through these pages, I'll see not only the work I've created, but also the person I was becoming while creating it.

If someone else finds a little inspiration here along the way, that's simply a beautiful bonus.
